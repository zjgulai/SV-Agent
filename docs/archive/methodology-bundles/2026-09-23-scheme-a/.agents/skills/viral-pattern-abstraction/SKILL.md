# Viral Pattern Abstraction

Read `docs/contexts/viral-replication/CONTEXT.md`, the workflow routing tree, and the relevant Content Performance evidence. A PatternHypothesis is a testable hypothesis, not a Creative Pattern approval or a winner formula.

## Interface

Input: `ReferenceBundle` and `DecompositionMap`. Output: `PatternHypothesis` with `primary_mechanism`, `invariant_elements`, `replaceable_elements`, `prohibited_surface_copy`, `adaptation_constraints`, evidence scope, and unknowns.

## Procedure

1. Restate the observed effect without causal language: “the reference presents X through Y within scope Z”.
2. Identify the smallest mechanism that would remain useful after changing product, person, wording, and surface media.
3. Put narrative relations, proof operations, experience cues, attention grammar, conversion actions, and trend conventions in separate fields. Do not collapse them into “style”.
4. Classify each reference element as invariant, replaceable, prohibited, or unknown. A protected or identity-bearing surface defaults to prohibited until rights review says otherwise.
5. Declare one primary mechanism. Treat all other mechanisms as secondary frozen context or split them into a separate Variant.
6. Attach every inference to an observation and record what the evidence cannot establish.

## Stop conditions

- the proposed pattern is only a copied script, prompt, shot list, or visual recipe;
- the hypothesis uses “viral formula”, “guaranteed”, “causes”, or universal language without a controlled experiment;
- the primary mechanism changes when a surface feature is replaced;
- rights, subject identity, or evidence scope is missing;
- the hypothesis requires an unsupported product claim to work.

## Completion criterion

The PatternHypothesis is complete only when an implementer can create a materially different target expression while preserving the declared mechanism, and a reviewer can name exactly which fields are allowed to change.
